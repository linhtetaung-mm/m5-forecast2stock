# From proposal to progress to the current project

The research question stayed the same. When does better demand forecasting improve replenishment across different sales regimes? The data are M5 recorded sales, the primary store is CA_1, and all 3,049 store–SKU series are retained. The main design change is to forecast the distribution of demand over the replenishment protection period and compare stocking policies on realized service and cost.

## Date provenance and the next decision

The submitted progress test ended February 25, 2016. The later `CPProj/final` and `inv-sim` single-store extensions scored February 26–March 24. The earlier network script loads raw sales through April 10 for panel coverage, but its last 28-day scored window starts February 26. Its eight-day diagnostic uses outcomes through March 31. I found no earlier April outcome-based model or policy selection in those recorded protocols. The current April forecast results have now been viewed in Part 2. For Part 3, select policy and service-matching rules on the policy-validation block and treat April as a fixed assessment rather than using it to tune.

The progress-stage lesson was about service, not just cheaper trajectories. The current forecasts do not answer the inventory question by themselves. Compare models at similar achieved service or along their cost–fill frontiers, and keep simulated M5 evidence separate from any claim about a retailer's actual demand, costs or realized savings.
